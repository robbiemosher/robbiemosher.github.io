Writing

Occasionally something occupies my attention for long enough that thinking about it stops being sufficient and I feel compelled to write it down.

Most of what ends up here lives somewhere around physics, mathematics, philosophy, and the places where those subjects start running into one another. These are not papers, nor do I intend them to be. They are mostly attempts to follow a question far enough that I understand what I actually think about it.

This tends to involve a few equations, several unnecessary detours, and occasionally discovering halfway through that the question I started with was not actually the interesting one.

On The Scale of Life

On the scales of life, humanity and the universe.

A wandering attempt to think about the rather peculiar scale at which we exist. Beginning with the second law of thermodynamics, it moves through time, space, gravity and information, comparing the physical limits of an individual human life with the much larger range of scales humanity has somehow learned to observe and understand.

Why Write Any of This?

Mostly because writing forces me to discover where the gaps in my own understanding are.

It is very easy to carry around an idea that feels perfectly coherent until you try to explain it from beginning to end. Then suddenly there is a missing step, an assumption you had forgotten you were making, or a word whose meaning becomes considerably less obvious once you actually have to define it.

Physics does this to me rather often.

So does philosophy.

I suspect that is why I keep coming back to both.
