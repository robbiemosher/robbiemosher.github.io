About Me

I'm Robbie Mosher, a physicist from Halifax, Nova Scotia. I completed my B.Sc. (Honours) in Physics at Saint Mary's University and am now beginning graduate studies in physics at McGill University in Montreal.

My interests are mostly in computational and theoretical physics, particularly statistical mechanics, condensed matter and materials modelling. My current research involves phase-field crystal models, which provide a way of studying crystalline materials over spatial and temporal scales that are difficult to access with fully atomistic approaches.

My undergraduate research was rather different. My honours thesis concerned the Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd quantum algorithm for solving systems of linear equations. Somewhere between quantum computing and crystalline materials, I developed a fairly general affection for problems where physics, mathematics and computation meet.

Outside Physics

Physics occupies a perhaps unreasonable amount of my attention, but not all of it. I'm interested in mathematics, philosophy, computing, history, and whatever other subject has managed to send me down a rabbit hole that week.

I particularly enjoy questions about how we reason about the world, how complicated behaviour emerges from simple rules, and how our intuitive picture of reality changes when we examine scales far outside ordinary human experience.

I also enjoy programming and tinkering with computers, reading, walking, coffee, video games, and occasionally writing the sorts of thoughts that eventually end up on this website.

A Little History

Before graduate school, I spent nearly five years working as a bartender in Halifax while completing my undergraduate degree. It was an unusual companion to studying physics, but a formative one. I spent a great deal of time moving between two rather different worlds: university classrooms and research on one side, and a busy restaurant bar on the other.

In 2026, I left Halifax for Montreal to begin the next part of that story at McGill. This website is partly a record of what follows.
