Research

My research interests are broadly in computational and theoretical physics, with a current focus on condensed matter, statistical mechanics, and computational materials modelling.

I am particularly interested in problems where relatively simple mathematical models give rise to complicated collective behaviour across many spatial and temporal scales.

Graduate Research

I am beginning graduate research at McGill University under the supervision of Prof. Nikolas Provatas. My work is centred on phase-field crystal modelling and its application to the dynamics of crystalline materials.

Phase-field crystal models describe a material using a continuous atomic-density field while retaining crystalline periodicity. This makes it possible to study phenomena associated with crystal structure and defects over spatial and temporal scales that can be difficult to reach using fully atomistic simulations.

My current work is still in its early stages. I am studying the theoretical foundations of PFC and related models, including their connections to classical density functional theory, statistical mechanics, and the Swift-Hohenberg model.

I am especially interested in how these methods can be used to study solidification, elasticity, defects, grain structure, and other emergent behaviour in crystalline materials.

Undergraduate Research

My undergraduate honours research at Saint Mary's University concerned the Harrow-Hassidim-Lloyd quantum algorithm, a quantum algorithm for solving systems of linear equations.

My thesis focused on the numerical behaviour of the algorithm and the relationship between its parameters and the fidelity of the resulting quantum state. In particular, I investigated the effects of phase estimation precision, evolution time, eigenvalue inversion, and the condition number of the input system.

I implemented numerical simulations of the algorithm in Python using PennyLane. The project gave me my first substantial experience working at the intersection of numerical computation, mathematical physics, and quantum information, and ultimately helped steer my interests toward computational approaches to physical problems more generally.

Research Interests

More broadly, I am interested in statistical mechanics, condensed matter physics, computational physics, nonlinear dynamics, numerical methods, and the mathematical structures underlying physical models. I also retain an interest in quantum mechanics and quantum information from my undergraduate work.

A recurring question behind much of what interests me is how behaviour at one scale emerges from dynamics at another: how microscopic rules become macroscopic structure, how collective behaviour arises from many interacting degrees of freedom, and how we can construct useful models when directly simulating every microscopic detail is impossible.
